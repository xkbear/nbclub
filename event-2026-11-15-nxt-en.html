NXT Build Sprint
Make something useful for someone real.

17 working projects from nine countries. NXT’s first youth hackathon began with a question: whose day could you make a little easier with something you build?

14–15 November 2026 · NZDTOnline · Discord · EnglishOrganiser: NXTSponsor: NewBee AI Club

Before opening a generation tool, find a person. See how they deal with the problem today.

That was NXT’s challenge—and the first scene required in each demo. One builder noticed a friend found exercise boring. Another saw a friend filling in job applications over and over. Others saw how much messaging it took to organise a simple outing. The starting point was a specific frustration, not a particular AI model.

NXT is a youth-led community of builders. NewBee AI Club sponsored its first Build Sprint, helping make participation free for young people worldwide and providing prizes for five award categories. NXT organised the event; judges scored the projects independently.

From an idea to a demo in one weekend

The event was open to ages 13–19, working solo or in teams of up to three. AI tools were allowed. NXT ran two opening briefings to accommodate different time zones. According to the organiser’s closing report, the build window lasted approximately 27 hours: 11:00 on 14 November to 14:00 on 15 November. Final presentations ran from 15:00 to 16:30 on 15 November, all in New Zealand Daylight Time. Times on the original registration page reflect the pre-event schedule.

Each submission needed a working project link, a screen-recorded demo of no more than two minutes, and short written answers. Demos had to show the real user’s existing workaround before presenting the new solution—not simply a slide deck about an idea.

  1. First round: 17 projects. Four judges reviewed the demos and written responses for usability, understanding of the user and problem, fit of the solution, and originality.
  2. Final round: eight projects. Three judges assessed the depth of the demo, design and experience, presentation, and Q&A. Finalists unable to attend live could participate through a recording and written answers.
  3. Scoring. Each round had four criteria scored from 1 to 10. The two rounds’ average judge scores were added for a total out of 80. Battle Towear ranked first in both rounds and won with 73.5 points.

The 17 valid projects came from India (five), the United States and New Zealand (three each), and the United Kingdom, Saudi Arabia, Australia, Bangladesh, France and Morocco (one each). Here are the eight finalists and the six projects that received awards.

Eight finalists. Who were they building for?

  • Battle Towear · FranceA pixel role-playing game for a friend who finds exercise boring. A phone recognises exercise movements, steps earn in-game rewards, and a food diary adds another everyday habit.
  • BLOCKED · New ZealandA multiplayer browser game on a local network for students who want to play together, with natural-disaster survival and Bed Wars modes.
  • Kata AI · AustraliaA Chrome automation extension for a friend repeatedly filling in job applications. It fills forms and can record new workflows.
  • SocialMaxing · United KingdomSmall daily challenges for a friend with social anxiety, recording how intimidating an action seemed beforehand and how it felt afterwards.
  • Squadup · BangladeshOne place for the venues, schedules, polls, budgets and carpool arrangements involved in organising an outing with friends.
  • TakTic · New ZealandA way to break large study tasks into small daily steps for students who struggle to get started.
  • Infinite Library · United StatesA platform to share interests, understand teammates better and discover opportunities to collaborate.
  • Backpack · United StatesA study tool for a friend juggling several demanding courses: photograph homework, generate a study plan and create relevant practice.

What worked—and what comes next

In one entirely online weekend, young people in different countries turned their peers’ real problems into working, demonstrable projects. The first event also produced practical lessons: announce special-award rules and submission questions in advance, close forms automatically at the deadline, arrange backup judges, and include special awards in the final announcements.

Thank you to NXT’s young organisers, the builders, the four judges and the community member who supported the Freebuff award. What NewBee supports is the opportunity for young people to find their own question and make their first version.
